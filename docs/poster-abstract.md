# Poster Abstract

## Concrete Bridge Deck and Slab Fatigue under Lane-Free Traffic

Assessment Using a Vehicle–Bridge Interaction Framework

**Wang Yanjun**  
Civil, Environmental and Resources Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Kyoto University

This study compares concrete bridge-deck fatigue performance under 22 traffic organizations using a coupled vehicle–bridge interaction model and a fixed fleet of 240 vehicles. The equivalent bridge model includes 26,520 bending/shear plate elements. Drawing-based nominal reinforcement sections recover concrete surface stresses from the saved dynamic moment histories. Compression-fatigue evaluation combines rainflow counting with an adopted concrete S-N relationship and cumulative damage, while tensile-threshold exceedances receive a separate assessment. At the duration-matched 40 m reference spacing, full-spectrum maximum damage equals 9.593 × 10⁻¹⁴ for two-lane traffic and 5.282 × 10⁻¹⁴ for grouped traffic. A 0.1 MPa range-cutoff sensitivity gives 2.978 × 10⁻¹⁵ and 9.546 × 10⁻¹⁵, respectively. Grouped traffic produces three tensile-exceedance locations and seven point episodes in the common observed set. Recorded stress ranges reach at least 98.53% of the interior field-range upper bound. The results demonstrate the need to interpret compression-fatigue damage together with tensile response and cycle-treatment assumptions. They describe one fleet passage under the specified loading and material-model assumptions, without predicting actual service life.
