# Poster Abstract

## Traffic Organization and Bridge Deck Response

**Wang Yanjun**  
Civil, Environmental and Resources Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Kyoto University

Vehicle–bridge interaction simulations can produce complete numerical results while leaving uncertainty about the spatial coverage of local response histories. This study examines traffic organization using an equivalent bridge model with 26,520 bending/shear plate elements and a fixed fleet of 240 vehicles. Twenty-two selected scenarios explore longitudinal spacing, lateral spread and vehicle ordering, including two-lane and grouped-traffic references. The workflow combines saved-result audits, contact-convergence checks and explicit recording maps. Five additional-output reruns expand the common interior observation map from 61 to 109 locations while reproducing every original moment history exactly. In a nominal gross-section stress-cycle screening comparison, the maximum fixed-channel I3 ratio to the two-lane reference decreases from approximately 11.6–11.8 to 1.76–1.79 for four uniform-order cases. The change reflects a larger sampling gain in the reference case, demonstrating that spatial coverage can materially affect a relative comparison even when the underlying histories remain consistent. A subsequent reinforcement-section recovery extension covers 119 saved coordinates and passes independent section-resultant checks. The results support a traceable numerical case study. They do not establish whole-deck fatigue maxima, experimental validity or absolute fatigue life.
