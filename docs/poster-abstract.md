# Poster Abstract

## Traffic Organization and Bridge Deck Response

**Wang Yanjun**  
Civil, Environmental and Resources Engineering, Faculty of Engineering, Kyoto University

This study examines the influence of traffic organization on concrete bridge-deck stress-cycle demand using a coupled vehicle–bridge interaction model. The equivalent bridge model includes 26,520 bending/shear plate elements and a fixed fleet of 240 vehicles. Twenty-two selected scenarios vary longitudinal spacing, normalized lateral spread and vehicle ordering, with two-lane and grouped-traffic references. Complete signed stress histories at fixed observation locations provide rainflow cycle counts and cumulative screening indices with exponents 3 and 5. Peak-range coverage reaches at least 95.53% across all selected cases. At the duration-matched 40 m reference spacing, uniform traffic with normalized lateral spread 0.15 gives relative maximum fixed-channel indices of 0.679 and 1.557, while grouped traffic gives 1.665 and 4.229. These results show that the interpretation of traffic-induced cycle demand depends on how strongly the index weights large cycles. Nominal reinforcement-section recovery covers the saved observation coordinates and passes independent section-resultant checks. The reported indices support comparison of the selected numerical scenarios. Experimental validity and absolute fatigue life remain open.
